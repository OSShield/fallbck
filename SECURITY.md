# Security Policy

## Supported Versions

We support major versions n-1 (except for 0.*), where security fixes will be backported to the previous stable version. 

Major versions generally contain breaking changes, major feature adjustments or shifts, while minor versions can contain feature additions that add, but do not modify existing functionality substantially.

Patch versions are generally only used for very minor bug fixes, grammar corrections, security fixes that do not change functionality.

| Version | Supported          |
| ------- | ------------------ |
| 1.x.x   | :white_check_mark: |
| 0.x.x   | :x:                |


## Reporting a Vulnerability

If you find any major vulnerabilities, we respectfully ask that you follow responsible disclosure guidelines below and report the vulnerability directly to our team at security@osshield.org.

While we cannot offer a bug bounty (yet), we will give you all the credit you deserve for identifying and disclosing the vulnerability (unlike Microsoft), and you are always more than welcome to post about it publicly after the agreed upon deadline. 

If you choose share contact information, we will acknowledge receipt of your report within 3 business days and propose a timeline for a coordinated disclosure.


## Responsible Research & Disclosure
Our guideline for responsible security research and disclosure details how we would ask that any security testing is performed in regards to our code and services.

For vulnerability testing we ask that:
- You notify us as soon as possible after you discover a real or potential security issue.
- You make every effort to avoid privacy violations, degradation of user experience, disruption to production systems, and destruction or manipulation of data.
- You only use exploits to the extent necessary to confirm a vulnerability’s presence. Do not use an exploit to compromise or exfiltrate data, establish persistent command line access, or use the exploit to pivot to other systems.
- You provide us a reasonable amount of time to resolve the issue before you disclose it publicly.
- You do not submit a high volume of low-quality reports with little to low impact.

We consider security research conducted in accordance with this policy to be authorized conduct under applicable computer fraud and abuse laws (such as the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act or equivalent regional statutes).

If you make a good-faith effort to comply with this policy during your security research, the OSShield Team and it's sponsoring companies commit to not:

- Pursue any legal or civil action against you.
- Report you to law enforcement authorities or initiate criminal complaints.
- Terminate or restrict your access to our products or services solely because of your research.

This safe harbor protection applies whether your actions might otherwise violate our Terms of Service, Acceptable Use Policy, or contractual restrictions, provided you remain within the defined scope and boundaries of this policy.
